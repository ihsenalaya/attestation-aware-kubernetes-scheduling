\section{Security Analysis}
\label{sec:security-analysis}

We analyse the six properties of \S\ref{sec:threat} against the adversaries
ADV-1--ADV-4, and tie each to the attack that would falsify it
(Table~\ref{tab:adversary}) and to a raw result.

\paragraph{Placement Safety.}
A sensitive pod binds only to a node carrying verified, fresh, non-revoked
evidence of the required TEE. ADV-1 presenting revoked (A3) or wrong-TEE (A4)
evidence is rejected at Filter; ADV-2/3 forging evidence (A10) is stopped by the
trust chain --- the node agent cannot write \texttt{AttestationEvidence}, and a
forged raw report is appraised to \texttt{unverified}. Empirically, A4 and A10
were each blocked in 30/30 runs on real AKS.

\paragraph{Evidence Freshness.}
Stale evidence is rejected at Filter (A2), and evidence that is revoked or
expires \emph{during} scheduling is caught by the fail-closed \texttt{PreBind}
re-check (A7). Because SEV-SNP evidence is necessary but time-bounded --- side
channels and transient compromise mean a node cannot be trusted forever
\cite{li2021cipherleaks} --- freshness and revocation are enforced rather than
assumed.

\paragraph{Identity Binding.}
The placement token binds pod UID, pod-spec hash, node identity, evidence and
policy hashes; mutating any bound field makes \texttt{verify-placement} fail
(A8, and the identity-binding experiment). A token minted for one pod cannot be
replayed for another.

\paragraph{Label Resistance.}
Attacker-forgeable metadata cannot grant placement: ADV-4 setting
\texttt{attested=true} without evidence (A1) leaves the pod \texttt{Pending} in
all runs, because the scheduler consults cryptographic evidence, not labels.

\paragraph{Runtime Consistency.}
A production-forbidden simulated runtime on a sensitive pod (A5), a missing
model digest (A5b), a policy modified between Filter and PreBind (A6), or a
simulated runtime class in production (A9) is refused at admission or at the
fail-closed PreBind re-check. The real AKS rerun blocks each Runtime Consistency
attack in 30/30 runs: A5 and A5b at admission, A6 via policy-hash mismatch at
PreBind, and A9 via production rejection of a labelled simulated
\texttt{RuntimeClass}.

\paragraph{Verifiable Placement.}
Every allowed placement carries an Ed25519 token that a third party validates
offline. This turns ``where did my workload run?'' from an assertion into a
checkable proof, and composes with scheduler self-security
(\S\ref{sec:scheduler-security}): a compromised node cannot forge evidence and a
rogue scheduler cannot forge a verifiable placement.

\paragraph{Out of scope.}
A malicious API server or etcd, a cloud provider that breaks the SEV-SNP
guarantee, micro-architectural side channels, physical attacks and signing-key
compromise are outside our model (\S\ref{sec:threat}); they are addressed by the
hardware TCB and orthogonal defences, not by orchestration-layer enforcement.
