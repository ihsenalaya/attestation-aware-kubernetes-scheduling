Cause
-----
The blocker is not Terraform, not os_sku, not GHCR/ACR, and not the DCASv5
vCPU quota number alone. The blocker is SKU exposure/availability for this
subscription in eastus2:

  DCASv5 quota exists (32 vCPU), but Standard_DC4as_v5 and Standard_DC8as_v5
  are not exposed by ARM Resource SKUs for eastus2 in this subscription, and
  AKS therefore rejects the node pool with VMSizeNotSupported.

This matches the Microsoft AKS CVM prerequisite: CVM sizes must be available
for the subscription in the cluster region, and sufficient quota is required.
Quota alone is necessary but not sufficient.

Solution options
----------------
1. Strict prompt-preserving path:
   Open Azure support / Confidential AKS enablement request for DCASv5 in
   eastus2. Ask Microsoft to expose/enable Standard_DC4as_v5 and/or
   Standard_DC8as_v5 for AKS in this subscription. Re-run only after ARM
   Resource SKUs returns the requested v5 size for eastus2.

2. Fastest technically viable path, if the article scope allows a documented
   migration from DCASv5 to DCasv6:
   Request quota for "Standard DCasv6 Family vCPUs" in eastus2, preferably 32
   vCPU. Then use Standard_DC4as_v6 with 8 nodes, or Standard_DC8as_v6 with 4
   nodes. ARM shows these v6 SKUs as ConfidentialComputingType=SNP and
   unrestricted in eastus2 for this subscription.

3. Do not use DCs_v3/DDCSv3 as a substitute for this paper unless the claims
   are rewritten. The current article claim is AMD SEV-SNP node-level AKS CVM.

Do not recreate AKS before one of the following is true:
- ARM Resource SKUs returns Standard_DC4as_v5 or Standard_DC8as_v5 for eastus2;
- or quota Standard DCasv6 Family vCPUs is increased and the paper explicitly
  accepts DCasv6 as the real SEV-SNP CVM family.
